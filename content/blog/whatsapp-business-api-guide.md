In India, customers often prefer WhatsApp to email, phone calls and website forms. For a business, that is an opportunity and a headache: leads arrive on several phones, follow-ups depend on memory, and nothing is recorded. The WhatsApp Business Platform, usually called the WhatsApp Business API, turns WhatsApp into a proper business channel connected to your CRM.

## WhatsApp Business app vs the WhatsApp Business Platform

| | WhatsApp Business app | WhatsApp Business Platform (API) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost | Free | Per-message pricing by Meta, plus your provider or software |
| Best for | Owner-run businesses | Teams, automation and scale |
| Users | Limited multi-device use | Many agents on one number, through software |
| Automation | Basic quick replies and away messages | Full automation, chatbots, triggers from your systems |
| CRM integration | Manual | Built in |
| Message records | On the phone | In your CRM or helpdesk |

If one person answers every chat, the app is fine. Once a team shares the work, or you want reminders and follow-ups sent automatically, you need the platform.

## How it works

You connect a phone number to the WhatsApp Business Platform, either directly through Meta's Cloud API or through a Business Solution Provider, and then to your CRM, website or helpdesk. Meta verifies your business, and your display name is reviewed.

## The two rules that shape everything

### 1. Templates for messages you start

When you message a customer first, the message must use a **template** that Meta has approved: for example, a booking reminder, an order update or a promotional offer. Templates can include variables (name, date, order number) and buttons.

### 2. The 24-hour customer service window

When a customer messages you, a **24-hour window** opens. Inside it, your team can reply freely: text, images, documents. Each new customer message resets the window. Outside it, you can only reach them with a template.

## Template categories and pricing

Meta groups templates into categories, mainly:

- **Marketing:** offers, announcements, re-engagement.
- **Utility:** updates about something the customer did, such as orders, bookings and payments.
- **Authentication:** one-time passwords.

Since **1 July 2025**, Meta charges **per delivered template message**, with rates that depend on the category and the recipient's country. Replies within the customer service window are not charged, and utility templates sent inside an open window are free. Rates change from time to time, so always check Meta's current rate card and your provider's fees.

## Opt-in and quality

WhatsApp requires **opt-in**: people must agree to hear from your business, and know what they are agreeing to. Good places to collect it are website forms, checkout, a click-to-WhatsApp ad or the first chat.

Your number has a **quality rating**, based on how people respond. Too many blocks and reports lower it and can limit how many people you can message. The fix is simple: send fewer, more relevant messages.

## What to automate first

1. **Instant lead replies** from website forms and ads, with the lead created in your CRM.
2. **Appointment and booking reminders**, which cut no-shows.
3. **Order and delivery updates.**
4. **Payment reminders and receipts.**
5. **Follow-up sequences** for leads who have gone quiet, within your opt-in.
6. **Routing:** assign each chat to the right person and show the full history.

## Connecting it to your CRM

The real value comes when WhatsApp is part of your sales system: every conversation logged against the lead, templates triggered by pipeline stages, and reports on which campaigns produce customers. ## Common mistakes

- Messaging bought or scraped number lists. This breaks the rules and damages your number.
- Sending marketing templates too often.
- No human hand-off when an automated flow cannot help.
- Keeping chats on personal phones, so the business loses the history when someone leaves.
