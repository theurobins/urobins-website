“How much for a website?” is the first question almost every business asks us, and the honest answer is: it depends on what you mean by a website. A five-page site for a clinic, an online store with 500 products and a customer portal with logins are all “websites”, and they are different projects.

This guide explains what actually drives the price in India, the costs that come every year after launch, and how to get quotes you can compare. We do not publish a price list, because two projects with the same page count can need very different work. What we can do is show you exactly where the money goes.

## Why are website quotes in India so different?

Because each quote describes different work. One freelancer may install a ready-made theme, swap in your logo and hand over the login. An agency may research your customers, design every page, write the copy, set up analytics and SEO, test on real phones and support the site for a year. Both are called “making a website”.

When two quotes differ by ten times, the scope almost always differs by ten times too. The only fair comparison is line by line.

## The eight things that set the price

### 1. The type of website

- **Business or brochure website:** a handful of pages explaining who you are, what you offer and how to contact you.
- **E-commerce store:** product catalogue, cart, checkout, payment gateway, shipping and order management.
- **Web application or portal:** user accounts, dashboards, data, permissions and integrations. This is software, priced like software.

### 2. The number of unique page designs

Ten pages built from one template cost far less than ten pages each designed for its own job. Count the *different* layouts you need, not the total pages.

### 3. Custom design or a template

A template is quick and affordable, but you look like every other site using it and you inherit its weight and limits. Custom design costs more up front and fits your brand, your customers and your content exactly.

### 4. Features and integrations

Each of these adds work: booking or appointment systems, payment gateways such as Razorpay, WhatsApp chat or automated messages, CRM integration, multiple languages (Hindi and English, for example), search and filters, member logins, and connections to accounting or inventory software.

### 5. Content

Who writes the words and supplies the photos? Copywriting, product descriptions, professional photography and video are real costs. If you provide them, the build is cheaper; if you expect the agency to, check it is in the quote.

### 6. SEO and speed groundwork

A site can be built to be found or not. Proper titles and descriptions, structured data, clean URLs, a sitemap, fast loading on mobile and Search Console setup take time. Skipping them makes a quote cheaper and the website far less useful. See our guide to [Core Web Vitals](/blog/core-web-vitals-explained/) for why speed matters.

### 7. Who builds it

Freelancers, small studios and full-service agencies price differently, and so do teams that design, build and host in-house versus those that outsource parts of the work. Ask who will actually do each part.

### 8. Support after launch

Is there a warranty period for bugs? Who updates plugins and fixes security issues? Who makes small content changes? Support can be included for a period, billed hourly or offered as a monthly plan.

## The costs that come every year

A website is not a one-time purchase. Plan for:

- **Domain renewal**, every year.
- **Hosting**, monthly or yearly, depending on traffic and the type of site.
- **SSL certificate**, often free with modern hosting.
- **Paid themes, plugins or apps**, many of which are yearly licences.
- **Maintenance**: security updates, backups, uptime monitoring and fixes.
- **Payment gateway fees** on each transaction for online stores.
- **Marketing**: SEO, ads and content to bring visitors to the site.

## How to get a quote you can compare

Send every agency the same short brief:

1. What your business does and who your customers are.
2. The pages or sections you need, and any features (booking, payments, logins).
3. Two or three websites you like, and why.
4. Who will provide the text and photos.
5. Your timeline and, if you have one, a budget range.

Then ask each one the same questions: what exactly is included, who owns the domain, hosting and code, what happens after launch, and how changes are charged. Our guide on [how to choose a web development company](/blog/how-to-choose-web-development-company/) lists the questions in full.

## Red flags in a cheap quote

- The agency registers the domain or hosting in *its* name.
- No mention of mobile testing, speed or SEO basics.
- “Unlimited revisions” with no defined scope.
- No written contract or payment milestones.
- A portfolio with no live links you can open.
