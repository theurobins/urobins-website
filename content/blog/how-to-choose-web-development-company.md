Choosing who builds your website or app is a bigger decision than choosing the platform. A good team turns a vague idea into something that brings in customers; a poor one leaves you with a slow site you cannot edit, on accounts you do not control. These are the questions we would ask if we were in your seat.

## 1. Can I see live projects?

Screenshots and mockups prove little. Ask for links to sites and apps that are live today, open them on your phone, and check how fast they load and how easy they are to use. Our own [portfolio](/portfolio/) links to every live project for this reason.

## 2. Who will actually do the work?

Many agencies sell the project and pass it to freelancers or another company. Ask who will design, who will code, who will test and who you will talk to every week. In-house teams usually mean fewer hand-offs and less lost context.

## 3. Who will own the domain, hosting, code and data?

The answer should be “you”. The domain and hosting accounts should be in your name, and the code should be in your repository or handed over in full. This single question prevents most horror stories.

## 4. What exactly is in scope, and what is not?

A good proposal lists pages, features and integrations, and also what is *excluded*. Vague scope leads to arguments later.

## 5. How will I see progress?

Weekly or fortnightly working builds you can click through on a staging link beat a single big reveal at the end. Ask how often you will see real progress.

## 6. What is the timeline, and what are the milestones?

Ask for a timeline tied to milestones, such as design approved, first working build and launch, with payments tied to the same milestones.

## 7. How do you handle changes?

Requirements change. Ask how change requests are estimated, approved and billed before work starts on them.

## 8. Will the site be fast and work on phones?

Most visitors in India arrive on a phone, often on mobile data. Ask how the team tests speed and which phones they test on. Our guide to [Core Web Vitals](/blog/core-web-vitals-explained/) explains what to look for.

## 9. Is SEO part of the build?

Titles, descriptions, structured data, a sitemap, clean URLs and Search Console setup should be part of the build, not an add-on after launch. If you are replacing an old site, ask how they will keep your Google rankings during the move.

## 10. What happens after launch?

Who fixes bugs, applies security updates, takes backups and makes small changes? Is there a warranty period? Is ongoing support a monthly plan or hourly?

## 11. Can I edit content myself?

Ask which parts of the site your team can edit without a developer, and ask for a short demo of the editor before you sign.

## 12. What does it really cost, including the yearly costs?

Ask for the build price and the running costs: domain, hosting, licences and maintenance. Our guide on [what a website costs in India](/blog/website-cost-india/) lists them all.

## Red flags to walk away from

- The domain or hosting will be registered in the agency's name.
- No written contract, or full payment demanded up front.
- “Unlimited revisions” instead of a defined scope.
- No live portfolio links.
- No mention of mobile, speed, security or SEO.
- Promises of guaranteed first-page Google rankings. Nobody controls Google.

## How to compare proposals

Put the proposals side by side and compare scope, ownership, timeline, support and process first, and price last. The cheapest quote often leaves out the work that makes a website useful.
