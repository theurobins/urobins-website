You type your company name into Google and it answers with “Showing results for” a different word. Your website, your Instagram and your Business Profile are nowhere. It feels like Google has never heard of you, and for now, that is exactly the problem.

It happened to us. In November 2026, a few weeks after we launched urobins.com, Google corrected “urobins” to “robins”, and other search engines took it for “urobilin”, a chemistry term. Nothing on the site was broken. Google simply had not yet learned that the word is a company.

## Why it happens

Google’s spelling correction is built from how words are used across the web and in searches. A word it has rarely seen, that looks close to a common word, is most likely a typo, so Google helps by correcting it.

A new brand name is a rare word by definition. Add a domain that was registered weeks ago, few or no links pointing to it, and pages that may not be indexed yet, and Google has almost no evidence that your name means something. This is not a penalty and nothing is wrong with your site. It is a lack of signals, and signals can be built.

## What fixes it

### 1. Get your pages indexed first

Google cannot connect a name to a website it has not indexed. Verify the site in [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console), submit your sitemap, and use **URL Inspection → Request indexing** on your home page and key pages. Our [checklist for getting a new site indexed](/blog/get-new-website-indexed-google/) walks through each step.

### 2. Say your name clearly where Google reads first

Put the exact brand name in the home page title, the main heading and the opening paragraph. Add Organization structured data with your `name`, your `url`, your logo, any genuine alternate spellings in `alternateName`, and every official profile in `sameAs`. This is how you tell search engines, in their own format, that these profiles and this website are one business.

### 3. Verify your Google Business Profile

Use exactly the same business name, website, phone number and city as on your site. An unverified profile does not appear publicly, even though you can see it while signed in.

### 4. Make every profile match

Your social profiles, directory listings and Business Profile should all use the same name, website address, phone number and email, ideally an address on your own domain rather than a free webmail account. Each one should link back to your website. Consistent details across many trusted sites are one of the strongest signals that a name refers to a real business.

### 5. Earn real mentions

Links and mentions from other sites teach Google that your name matters: clients crediting you in their website footer, partners, local news, industry directories and associations, and your team listing the company on their own LinkedIn profiles. A handful of genuine mentions on sites Google already trusts is worth more than hundreds of low-quality listings.

### 6. Let people search for you

Put the name on your invoices, email signature, business cards, packaging and social bios. Every real person who searches for your brand and clicks your website is evidence that the word is not a typo.

## What not to do

- **No pages for each misspelling.** Separate pages for “U Robins”, “You Robins” and so on are treated as doorway pages. Mention common misspellings naturally once, for example in an FAQ about how to pronounce the name.
- **No hidden text or keyword stuffing.** Repeating the brand name dozens of times does not help and can hurt.
- **No bought clicks or bot traffic.** Fake searches are easy for Google to spot and risk your site’s standing.
- **No made-up addresses.** A fake office address can get your Business Profile suspended. If you work from a home office or have no office yet, list your service area instead.

## How long it takes

For most new businesses, the correction fades over a few weeks to a few months after the site is indexed, depending on how many real mentions and branded searches build up. In the meantime, searches that include your domain or your city often surface the site sooner. Keep publishing useful pages, keep your details consistent, and check the Search Console **Performance** report for your brand name: the first impressions are a good sign the correction is on its way out.
