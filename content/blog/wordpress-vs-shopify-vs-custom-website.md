Most business owners we talk to have already been told three different things: “just use Shopify”, “WordPress can do everything” and “you need a custom website”. Each is right for some businesses and wrong for others. This guide explains what each option is good at, what it costs to keep running and how to choose.

## The short answer

| If your main need is… | Choose |
| --- | --- |
| Selling products online with minimal technical work | **Shopify** |
| Publishing and editing lots of content yourself, with common features | **WordPress** |
| Unusual features, integrations, user accounts or maximum speed | **A custom build** |

## Shopify: the store that runs itself

Shopify is a hosted e-commerce platform. You pay a monthly subscription, and Shopify handles hosting, security and checkout. You choose a theme, add products and connect a payment gateway.

**Best for:** brands whose website is mainly a shop. Two of our own projects, [Swarica](/portfolio/swarica/) and [Ajar Organics](/portfolio/ajar-organics/), run on Shopify for exactly this reason: the teams can add products and run promotions without a developer.

**Watch for:**
- Monthly subscription plus paid apps, which add up as you grow.
- Transaction and payment gateway fees on each sale.
- Less freedom for unusual features; you work within Shopify's structure.
- Content-heavy sites (guides, a big blog) are possible but not Shopify's strength.

## WordPress: flexible content, lots of plugins

WordPress is open-source software you install on your own hosting. It powers a very large share of the web, has thousands of themes and plugins, and WooCommerce adds e-commerce.

**Best for:** businesses that publish a lot, such as blogs, guides, news and service pages, and want to edit everything themselves, using features that well-maintained plugins already cover.

**Watch for:**
- You (or your agency) are responsible for hosting, backups and security updates.
- Every plugin is code from a different author; too many slow the site and widen the attack surface.
- Page builders make editing easy but often make pages heavy and slow on phones.

## A custom website: built for exactly what you need

A custom site is written for your business, typically with a modern framework such as Next.js or Astro, often with a headless CMS so your team can still edit content.

**Best for:**
- Web applications: customer portals, dashboards, marketplaces, booking systems.
- Sites that must integrate deeply with a CRM, ERP or your own software.
- Brands where speed, design freedom and security are priorities.

Our property platform [95Acre](/portfolio/95acre/) and the [Couponbaazi](/portfolio/couponbaazi/) website are custom builds, because their search, listings and product stories needed more than a template could give.

**Watch for:**
- Higher up-front cost than a theme.
- You need a reliable team to maintain it; insist on documented code you own.

## Running costs compared

| | Shopify | WordPress | Custom |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hosting | Included in subscription | You pay a host | You pay a host or cloud |
| Platform fee | Monthly subscription | None (open source) | None |
| Extras | Paid apps | Paid themes and plugins | Built as needed |
| Updates and security | Handled by Shopify | Your responsibility | Your team's responsibility |
| Design freedom | Within themes | High, with effort | Complete |

## A simple way to decide

Answer three questions:

1. **Is the website mostly a shop?** If yes, start with Shopify.
2. **Will you publish and edit a lot of content, using standard features?** If yes, WordPress is a good fit.
3. **Do you need something a plugin or app cannot do well, or a portal with logins?** Then build custom.

If you answered “yes” to more than one, that is normal. Many businesses run a Shopify store alongside a custom site or a WordPress blog. The right mix depends on where your revenue comes from.

## Whatever you choose, own it

Make sure the domain, the hosting or platform account, the code and your customer data are in your name. Switching platforms later is very doable, provided every old URL redirects to its new page so you keep your search rankings.
