“When can we launch?” is the question behind every app project. The honest answer depends on scope, but the phases are predictable, and so are the things that cause delays. Here is a realistic timeline for a typical business app.

## The short answer

- **Focused MVP** (one main user journey, a backend and an admin panel): about **6–8 weeks** to production.
- **Full-featured app** (several user roles, payments, integrations): **3–6 months**, ideally released in phases.
- **Complex platforms** (marketplaces, real-time features, heavy integrations): longer, delivered as a series of releases.

At Urobins we aim for a production-ready MVP in about 45 days for a typical product, with a test build on your phone every two weeks along the way.

## The phases

### 1. Discovery (1–2 weeks)

Who are the users, what is the one job the app must do brilliantly, and what is out of scope for version one? Discovery ends with a feature list, a technical plan and a release plan. Skipping it is the most expensive way to save time.

### 2. Design (1–3 weeks, overlapping the build)

User flows, screens and a clickable prototype you can tap through on your own phone. Fixing a flow in a prototype takes minutes; fixing it in code takes days.

### 3. Build (4–10 weeks for an MVP)

Front end and backend move together in short sprints: APIs, database, sign-in, notifications, the admin panel and the app screens. A test build should reach your phone regularly, through TestFlight for iOS and a test track on Google Play.

### 4. Testing (throughout, plus a final round)

Testing on real devices, including older and low-end Android phones common in India, on slow networks, with large text and other accessibility settings. Crash reporting and analytics go in before launch, not after.

### 5. Store launch (allow 1–3 weeks)

- **App Store:** listing, screenshots, privacy details and review. Apple reviews most submissions within a day or two, but first submissions and apps that need changes can take longer.
- **Google Play:** personal developer accounts created after 13 November 2023 must run a **closed test with at least 12 testers, opted in continuously for 14 days**, before they can apply for production access. Start that test early, or use an organisation account if your business qualifies.

## What actually slows apps down

1. **Scope that keeps growing.** Every “small addition” pushes launch back. Keep a list for version two.
2. **Slow feedback.** A week waiting for design approval is a week lost.
3. **Late content and accounts.** Store accounts, payment gateway approvals, legal pages and product data take time to arrange.
4. **Third-party integrations.** Payment, logistics or government APIs often need approvals and testing.
5. **No one owning decisions.** One decision-maker on your side speeds everything up.

## How to launch sooner

- Define the **one core journey** the MVP must nail.
- Use **Flutter** when the app fits it. One codebase for both stores saves weeks; see [Flutter vs native](/blog/flutter-vs-native-app-development/).
- Set up the **developer accounts** (Apple and Google) in week one.
- Review a **working build** every week or two and give feedback quickly.
- Plan **version 2** before launch, so good ideas are not lost and launch is not delayed.

## After launch

An app is never finished. Plan for crash fixes, updates for new iOS and Android versions, new features from real user feedback, and store optimisation. The first month after launch is when you learn the most.
