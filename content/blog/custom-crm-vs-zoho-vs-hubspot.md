Every growing business reaches the point where spreadsheets, sticky notes and WhatsApp chats stop working as a sales system. Leads go cold, follow-ups are missed and nobody knows the real pipeline. A CRM fixes that. The question is which kind.

## The short answer

- **Standard sales process, small to mid-sized team:** use an off-the-shelf CRM.
- **Unusual process, many users or deep integration needs:** consider a custom CRM.
- **Somewhere in between, which is most businesses:** an off-the-shelf CRM with custom integrations and automation.

## What off-the-shelf CRMs do well

**Zoho CRM** is popular in India for its value and its large family of connected apps for accounting, support and HR. **HubSpot** is known for being easy to use and for strong marketing and email tools. **Salesforce** is the most extensive and customisable, typically chosen by larger organisations with an admin team.

All of them give you, quickly:

- Contacts, companies, deals and a visual pipeline.
- Email integration, tasks and reminders.
- Reports and dashboards.
- Mobile apps for the sales team.
- Large ecosystems of integrations.

## When off-the-shelf starts to hurt

- **Your process does not fit.** You bend the way you sell to match the software, or keep a spreadsheet beside the CRM.
- **Per-user pricing grows.** Costs rise with every seat, and add-ons for features you need.
- **Integration gaps.** Your own app, ERP, inventory or billing system does not connect cleanly.
- **Data you cannot shape.** Custom objects, approvals or field-level permissions are limited or expensive.
- **Low adoption.** The team finds it cluttered and goes back to WhatsApp.

## When a custom CRM pays off

A CRM built for your business makes sense when:

1. Your workflow is genuinely different, as in real estate site visits, education admissions, field sales or multi-step approvals.
2. You have many users, so per-seat pricing outweighs the cost of building.
3. The CRM must work closely with your own product, app or operations data.
4. You need full control of your data, hosting and security.

The trade-off: a custom CRM costs more up front and needs a reliable team to maintain it. Insist on documented code and data you own.

## The hybrid most businesses need

Often the best answer is not either-or. Keep Zoho, HubSpot or Salesforce, and add:

- **Lead capture** from your website, ads, WhatsApp and marketplaces into one pipeline, de-duplicated.
- **Automation**: assignment, follow-up sequences, reminders and quotes.
- **WhatsApp messaging** through the official API. See our [WhatsApp Business API guide](/blog/whatsapp-business-api-guide/).
- **Two-way sync** with your accounting, inventory or app database.
- **Dashboards** that combine CRM data with marketing and revenue.

## How to decide

1. Write down your sales process step by step, including the exceptions.
2. Trial one or two off-the-shelf CRMs with your *real* pipeline for two weeks.
3. List what did not fit and what it would cost per user per year as you grow.
4. If the gaps are integrations, go hybrid. If the gaps are the core workflow, consider custom.

## Adoption is everything

The best CRM is the one your team actually uses. Keep fields to the minimum, automate data entry, give salespeople a fast mobile view, and review usage a few weeks after launch.
