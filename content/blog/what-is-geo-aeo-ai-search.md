People increasingly ask AI tools instead of typing keywords into Google: “best web development company in Bengaluru”, “how much does an app cost in India”, “which CRM for a small business”. The answers come from ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot and Google's AI Overviews, and they cite a handful of sources. Being one of those sources is the goal of AEO and GEO.

## SEO, AEO and GEO in one minute

| | Goal | Where it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **SEO**, search engine optimisation | Rank in search results | Google and Bing results pages |
| **AEO**, answer engine optimisation | Be *the* direct answer | Featured snippets, “People also ask”, voice assistants |
| **GEO**, generative engine optimisation | Be cited inside AI-written answers | ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot, AI Overviews |

They are layers, not alternatives. AI tools still find pages through search indexes and their own crawlers, so a site that fails at SEO rarely succeeds at GEO.

## Where GEO comes from

The term comes from a research paper, *GEO: Generative Engine Optimization*, by researchers from Princeton and other institutions, first published in 2023 and presented at KDD 2024. The authors tested ways of rewriting content and measured how visible it became in AI-generated answers. They reported visibility gains of up to 40%, and found that what works varies by topic. Adding citations to sources, quotations and statistics were among the methods tested. The lesson for businesses: content that states facts clearly and backs them up is easier for AI systems to use.

## How AI search chooses sources

Each system works differently, but the pattern is similar:

1. The question is turned into one or more searches.
2. The system retrieves pages from a search index or its own crawl.
3. It reads the most relevant passages and writes an answer.
4. It cites the pages it relied on.

So, to be cited, a page must be **crawlable**, **indexed**, **relevant** to the question, and contain a **passage that answers it clearly**.

## What to do: the practical checklist

### Make sure AI crawlers can read your site

- Do not block AI search crawlers such as **OAI-SearchBot** (ChatGPT search), **PerplexityBot** and **Claude-SearchBot** in robots.txt if you want to appear in their answers.
- Get indexed by **Google and Bing**. Bing's index feeds several assistants. See [how to get a new site indexed](/blog/get-new-website-indexed-google/).
- Serve real HTML. Content that only appears after heavy JavaScript runs is harder for crawlers to read.

### Write answers, not just pages

- Use the **questions customers ask** as headings.
- Put a **direct, self-contained answer** of 40 to 60 words right under each question, then the detail.
- Start long articles with **key takeaways**: they are exactly the kind of passage AI answers quote.
- Use **tables** for comparisons and **numbered steps** for processes.

### State facts plainly, and back them up

- Say who you are, where you are and what you do in simple sentences on your home, about and service pages.
- Cite sources for claims, and use real data you can stand behind. Never invent statistics.
- Keep facts consistent everywhere: name, address, phone, services.

### Use structured data

Organization, LocalBusiness, Service, Article, FAQPage and BreadcrumbList markup describe your business and pages in a format machines read directly. Link your official profiles with `sameAs`.

### Build a brand AI systems recognise

AI answers favour businesses that are mentioned across the web: directories, reviews, press, partner sites, social profiles. A verified Google Business Profile and consistent listings matter here too. If Google still treats your brand as a typo, read [why Google corrects new brand names](/blog/google-did-you-mean-new-brand-name/).

### Consider llms.txt

**llms.txt** is a proposed standard: a Markdown summary of your site at `/llms.txt`. It is cheap to add, and some AI tools read it, but support is uneven and Google has said it does not use it for Search. Treat it as a bonus, not a strategy.

## How to measure it

- Referral traffic from chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, copilot.microsoft.com and gemini.google.com in Google Analytics 4.
- Ask the assistants your customers' questions regularly and note who gets cited.
- Branded searches and direct traffic over time.

## What not to do

- Don't publish hundreds of thin AI-written pages. Google treats this as scaled content abuse, and AI tools do not cite it.
- Don't hide text or stuff keywords for bots.
- Don't trust anyone who guarantees ChatGPT mentions.
